# Communication — Training Budget Effectiveness Analysis

## The One-Sentence Answer

> Of $1,675,886 in training spend, only **$492,343 (29.4%)** is fully defensible — plausibly recorded **and** completed. The 80% target cannot be reached by changing programmes, because the root cause is **enrolment control**, not training quality.

The headline number deliberately is not the 50.6% effectiveness rate. It is the 29.4% that survives both tests at once, which comes from cross-tabulating record integrity against completion — a split neither metric shows on its own.

## Narrative Structure

Built for a CFO audience — no p-values, no statistical terminology, every finding tied to a dollar figure.

### Situation
$1,675,886 spent across 3,000 sessions in one fiscal year. Management targets 80% effectiveness within 6 months, up from 50.6%.

### Three findings

**1. Half the budget produces no completion — and it is chronic.**
$828,402 ends in *Failed* or *Incomplete*. Across all 12 months, effectiveness stayed between 44% and 59% and never approached target. There is no single incident to point at.

**2. A third of the budget is booked against people who were not working.**
1,029 sessions ($574,242) are dated after the employee's exit — a median of 358 days after, with the furthest case 4.6 years later. Adding the 288 sessions dated before employees were hired, **$735,145 (43.9%)** sits on records that cannot be true.

**3. Changing programmes barely helps.**
Six possible drivers were tested. Only programme choice showed any effect, and the spread is just 10 points between best and worst. Delivery type, duration, cost, tenure and department showed no difference at all. Expensive training is not completed more often than cheap training.

### Why the target is unreachable
Running every available fix simultaneously moves effectiveness from 50.6% to **53.4%** — roughly 3 of the 29.4 points required. The remaining **26.6 points** trace to factors the L&D system has never recorded: instructor quality, course relevance, and participant workload.

### The measurement trap
Stopping $735,145 of spend on non-employees raises measured effectiveness by only **1.8 points**. The largest available saving is nearly invisible in the metric being targeted. Chasing 80% would let Bellwood report a good number while continuing to pay for training on behalf of people who left years ago.
